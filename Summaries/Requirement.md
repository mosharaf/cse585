# Requirements for CSE 585 Lecture Summary

## Submission

Submit your summary as a single Google Doc.
Share it with [cse585-staff@umich.edu](mailto:cse585-staff@umich.edu) as Commenter, then email the link to the same address.
At the top of the document, list the full names and unique names of all team members, and the lecture date.

**The summary must be emailed to the instructor team within 24 hours of the presentation.**
**Late summaries will not be graded.**


## AI-Assisted Lecture Summary

Write a summary of the **research area** your assigned lecture covers.
You are required to use AI tools of your choice to help with this.

Your submission should contain the following sections:

| Section | Name | AI Usage Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Research direction of the field | AI-assisted |
| 2 | Lecture papers | AI-assisted |
| 3 | Lecture discussions | Manual |
| 4 | Proposal for follow-up work | AI-assisted |
| 5 | A reflection on AI usage | Manual |
| 6 | A transcript of your conversation with AI | AI |

### Section 1: Research direction of the field

Each lecture covers several papers from a shared research area. Summarize that area by addressing:
* What is the research problem, and why is it important?
* What was the state of related work *before* the lecture papers appeared?
* At a high level, what do the lecture papers solve, and where do they stand in the area?
* If applicable, what follow-up work has built on the lecture papers?

Do not simply list what each related work does.
A good summary should contain the how related works' ideas contribute to the evolution of the field.


### Section 2: Lecture papers

Summarize each *required* paper discussed in the lecture, focusing on the technical details of its problem and solution.

Address the following aspects in bold text.
The guidelines under each are optional starting points.
* **What is the problem of this paper?**
  * Is the problem important and/or hard?
  * What have existing solutions missed?
  * Does the paper provide sufficient theoretical or experimental evidence for the problem?
* **What is the solution of this paper?**
  * What key insight drives the solution?
  * What assumptions does it rely on — do they reduce the problem reasonably, or oversimplify it?
  * What are the tradeoffs of the solution?
* **How is the solution evaluated?**
  * Is the evaluation set up correctly? What hardware, workload, and implementation are involved?
  * What properties are evaluated, and are they tied to the problem?
  * What results are expected, and what are unexpected?
* **Overall, what are 1-2 shortcomings of this paper?**


### Section 3: Lecture discussions

Summarize the main questions raised and discussed in the lecture, in Q&A form or any format you prefer.
Per [FERPA](https://safecomputing.umich.edu/protect-privacy/privacy-u-m/videoconferencing/recording-privacy-concerns),
you may not record the lecture, so pay attention and capture the main points as they come up.


### Section 4: Proposal for follow-up work

Propose a new project in this field.
Using the survey from Section 1, identify an open problem and propose a solution to it.
This should be at most 3 short paragraphs.
However, it should reflect careful thought about the field's landscape.
Evaluate your solution with the same rigor as in your paper summaries.
You may treat this as a chance to draft a short version of your group project proposal.


### Section 5: A reflection on AI usage

Sections 1, 2, and 4 require AI assistance — surveying a whole field quickly is impractical without it.

Summarize your experience using AI for these sections, reflecting on where it helped and where it did not.
Cite concrete examples from Section 6.


### Section 6: A transcript of your conversation with AI

Provide a **complete and unmodified** transcript of your multi-turn conversation(s) with your AI tool.
Label the model (e.g., GPT-6 Astra), tool (e.g., ChatGPT Desktop), and the date(s) of the conversation.
If multiple students start multiple AI sessions, concatenate all of them.

Start Section 6 on a new page and paste it as plain text.
Use distinct text styles to distinguish prompts from AI responses.


## Guidelines

Be creative in your conversation with the AI. Some ideas (not exhaustive):

* Give the AI a short spec of how it should summarize the paper
* Ask the AI detailed technical questions you had trouble understanding
* Ask about the paper's relationship to another paper
* State that you believe the field is unimportant, and ask what the AI thinks
* Ask the AI's opinion on a question raised in the lecture

You are free to choose the number of sessions to have with the AI and what context to provide.

Sections 1-5 must be under 3 pages of Google Doc, 12pt, single-spaced. We will not read beyond 3 pages.
Section 6 has no length limit, so submit the complete and unmodified conversation.

Your submission will be public on GitHub throughout the semester.
One purpose of this assignment is for students and the instructor to quickly pick up a lecture's discussion by reading your summary.
It would be a waste of everyone's time to submit a simple prompt and massive AI-generated slop.

## Best Summary Award

Near the end of the semester, you will vote on the best summary among all lectures.
All members of the Best Summary Award team will receive 2 bonus points for this course.
